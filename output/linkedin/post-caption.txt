I’ve been working on DemandTwin, a platform that brings data science, probabilistic consumer modeling, and full-stack engineering into one product research workflow.

The goal is to explore how product attributes, pricing, and competing alternatives affect consumer choices before a founder commits to production.

The data science work includes:

• Data acquisition and EDA — reproducible ingestion, source checksums, missing-value analysis, duplicate removal, cancellation handling, and Parquet storage. The UCI pipeline processed 541,909 source records and retained 348,872 UK purchase lines under documented filters.

• Feature engineering and leakage prevention — recency, frequency, spend, and product activity features, with disjoint customers and separate temporal snapshots for evaluation.

• Machine learning — Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost for an external repeat-purchase benchmark, plus conditional logit with an explicit no-purchase alternative for choice studies.

• Probabilistic modeling — empirical Bayes segment preferences, Laplace uncertainty intervals, and an offline hierarchical Bayesian model using PyMC with convergence and posterior predictive checks.

• Evaluation and explainability — log loss, Brier score, ROC-AUC where appropriate, calibration plots, SHAP summaries, and model-based feature contributions.

• Adaptive experiments — expected information gain, sequential preference updates, task budgets, and segment enrollment constraints.

• Reproducibility — recorded seeds, dataset and feature versions, model artifacts, and MLflow experiment tracking.

For the UK repeat-purchase benchmark, Logistic Regression achieved a held-out ROC-AUC of 0.655 and log loss of 0.608. A separate Twin-2K-500 benchmark explored repeated hypothetical pricing answers from a US survey panel.

The application connects these research workflows to product management, consumer experiments, scenario comparisons, and PDF reports using Next.js, FastAPI, PostgreSQL, Redis, and Celery.

The screenshots show a clearly labeled synthetic snack-choice demo. Prospective validation with Indian consumers and observed purchase evidence is the next research milestone.

I’d welcome feedback from people working in data science, consumer research, and applied machine learning.
