# Literature review

- **Train (2009)** develops logit, mixed logit, simulated estimation and individual-level Bayesian parameters with worked choice examples. Mixed logit permits heterogeneous preferences and a more flexible substitution structure. The methods motivate explicit alternatives, an outside option and probability integration in DemandTwin. They do not supply snack-market calibration. [Author-hosted second edition](https://eml.berkeley.edu/books/choice2.html)
- **McFadden and Train (2000)** establish the approximation properties of mixed multinomial logit under random utility and discuss estimation. This methodological result motivates random coefficients. The choice sets and actual-population mixture still need identification from the study. [Authors’ working paper](https://eml.berkeley.edu/wp/mcfadden1198/mcfadden1198.pdf)
- **Foster et al. (2019)** develop variational approximations to expected information gain and demonstrate them on design problems. Their objective motivates comparing posterior uncertainty before and after a response. The DemandTwin implementation uses direct posterior draws and entropy differences for a finite set of candidates. Performance depends on posterior approximation and the chosen candidate bank. [Official preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.05480)
- **Twin-2K-500 (2025)** provides longitudinal survey profiles from 2,058 US respondents for digital-twin evaluation. It supports research on held-out person-level answers. A US survey panel provides no direct evidence for Mumbai snack preferences. [Official paper record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17479)

The open research question in this project is whether aligned, evidence-specific calibration and constrained task assignment improve predictive fidelity in a prospective local snack study.
